Operon: Roadmap Complete

Eight Phases, Six Layers, 1130 Tests, and Integration with Operational Runtimes

Bogdan Banu · March 2026 · github.com/coredipper/operon

Release: v0.23.1
Abstract

The eight-phase roadmap that started with bi-temporal memory in v0.19 is complete. Operon has expanded from typed wiring patterns into a stack covering auditable memory, adaptive template selection, sleep consolidation, social learning, curiosity signals, and developmental staging with critical periods. This post covers what the Phase 8 integration tests verify, how the subsystems operate together across an organism’s lifecycle, and what it takes to connect this structural layer to operational runtimes like Swarms and AnimaWorks.

The progression (structure → memory → adaptation → cognition → development → integration) follows a biological sequence: fixed structure first (genome), then learned bias (epigenetics), then plastic and eventually crystallizing development. Each layer depends on the stability of the one beneath it, and Phase 8 tests whether they compose without interfering with one another.

2. Cross-Subsystem Integration

Subsystems that pass unit tests in isolation can still clash when wired together. Phase 8 adds five end-to-end integration tests to verify that a watcher can monitor a substrate-equipped organism, that sleep consolidation distills adaptive runs cleanly, and that teacher-learner scaffolding enforces developmental gates.

Substrate + Watcher

An organism runs with both a bi-temporal memory substrate and a watcher attached. Stages emit facts into the substrate via emit_output_fact while the watcher collects signals and checks convergence. Afterward, the bi-temporal store holds the auditable fact trail and the watcher records its per-stage signal history. Both read and write shared_state without interfering because they use disjoint keys.

Adaptive Assembly + Consolidation

An AdaptiveSkillOrganism fingerprints a task, retrieves the top-scoring template from PatternLibrary, assembles and runs it, and records the outcome. SleepConsolidation then replays the successful run record into EpisodicMemory, promotes it from WORKING to EPISODIC tier, and compresses recurring patterns into consolidated templates. Across both stages, the experience pool accumulates intervention outcomes, closing the loop from execution and recording through consolidation back to future template selection.

Social Learning + Development

A MATURE organism (80% telomere consumed) exports its successful templates, and an EMBRYONIC learner with an open “rapid adoption” critical period imports them through scaffold_learner(). Templates marked min_stage="adolescent" are filtered out until the learner is ticked forward to ADOLESCENT, at which point the same scaffolding call admits them and the critical period closes permanently. Trust scores then update based on whether the adopted templates work for the learner.

Memory Adapters

histone_to_bitemporal() converts HistoneStore markers into bi-temporal facts, and episodic_to_bitemporal() does the same for EpisodicMemory entries. Both adapters are one-way and non-destructive, letting epigenetic bias, episodic recall, and auditable facts feed into a single temporal store for unified querying. When a consolidation cycle promotes histone marks, it can now emit bi-temporal facts alongside them so the promotion itself is auditable.

Shared Interfaces Across Phases

The integration tests confirm that the core abstractions held across all eight phases without redesign. Phase 2’s three-layer context model (topology, ephemeral, bi-temporal) still sits underneath Phase 8 unchanged. Developmental signals in Phase 7 attach through the same SkillRuntimeComponent protocol introduced in Phase 3, and both sleep consolidation and social learning operate directly on Phase 3’s PatternLibrary scoring function. Keeping those interfaces fixed from the start is what makes cross-subsystem composition straightforward.

3. End-to-End Organism Lifecycle

With all eight phases wired together, an organism moves through a full lifecycle from initialization to maturation.

It starts at the EMBRYONIC stage with maximum learning plasticity and minimal tool access. While its initial critical period is open, it adopts templates from mature peers via trust-weighted social learning. As its telomere ticks down through JUVENILE and ADOLESCENT stages, progressively more complex tools gated by Plasmid.min_stage become available. Throughout execution, the watcher tracks epistemic signals (epiplexity, curiosity), somatic signals (ATP budget, developmental maturity), and species-specific signals (immune threats), intervening with retry, escalate, or halt when thresholds trip. If curiosity spikes while a stage runs on a fast model, the watcher escalates to the deep model for closer analysis.

Between batches of runs, SleepConsolidation prunes stale context, replays successful patterns into episodic memory with tier promotion, compresses recurring high-success patterns into consolidated templates, runs counterfactual replay over bi-temporal corrections (“what if we had known this fact earlier?”), and promotes frequently accessed histone marks from temporary ACETYLATION to permanent METHYLATION. Meanwhile, the experience pool accumulates intervention outcomes across runs so the watcher calibrates its decisions against operational history.

Once it reaches the MATURE stage, the organism can act as a teacher, exporting proven templates to younger peers filtered by each learner’s developmental readiness. The trust registry tracks whether those exports help the recipient, building a calibrated peer reputation. With its own critical periods closed, the mature organism stops rapid template adoption and relies on its established repertoire, while bi-temporal memory preserves every fact, correction, and historical belief state so past decisions can be reconstructed.

4. Connecting to Operational Runtimes

Operon is a structural library rather than an agent runtime: it specifies how agents are wired, what they record, how they adapt, and how their capabilities mature, leaving execution and hosting to other systems. The natural next step is connecting this structural layer to operational runtimes.

Two existing projects cover the complementary halves of the stack. AnimaWorks builds persistent agent organizations running 24/7 with identity, memory consolidation, and heartbeat-driven autonomy. That covers the long-lived cognitive runtime Operon omits, though it lacks structural guarantees, bi-temporal auditability, and convergence detection. Swarms handles enterprise multi-agent orchestration with 10+ pattern types and production scaling, providing production deployment infrastructure while leaving out formal topology analysis, an experience pool, and developmental gating.

Together, they partition cleanly into three layers:

  • Operon (structural layer): topology advice, epistemic analysis, pattern scoring, watcher signals, bi-temporal auditability, and developmental gating.
  • Swarms (orchestration layer): enterprise deployment, scaling, monitoring, and multi-provider routing.
  • AnimaWorks (cognitive layer): persistent identity, 6-channel priming, heartbeat autonomy, memory consolidation, and a forgetting lifecycle.

Each layer works independently, with Operon validating the structure, Swarms executing it at scale, and AnimaWorks supplying persistent state and autonomy.

Completing the roadmap opens four concrete integration points:

  • Template exchange: Operon’s SocialLearning can import templates from Swarms’ AutoSwarmBuilder output, score them in the PatternLibrary, and share successful ones with AnimaWorks organizations via trust-weighted adoption.
  • Convergence detection: Operon’s watcher can monitor AnimaWorks’ heartbeat cycle and Swarms’ workflow execution, applying the BIGMAS intervention-rate signal to detect non-convergence in external runtimes.
  • Consolidation bridging: AnimaWorks’ nightly memory consolidation can feed into Operon’s SleepConsolidation via the memory adapters, creating auditable bi-temporal records from AnimaWorks’ episodic and semantic memories.
  • Developmental scaffolding: a mature Operon organism can scaffold a newly deployed AnimaWorks agent, gating which templates and tools are available based on the new agent’s developmental stage and critical period windows.

Scope of the Library

Keeping Operon focused on structural guarantees lets operational runtimes adopt only the parts they lack. Without bi-temporal logging, an AnimaWorks agent consolidating memories cannot reconstruct why it made earlier decisions; without scored templates, a Swarms workflow repeats failed patterns; without developmental gating, a newly deployed agent receives full tool access before demonstrating competence. Operon supplies those constraints as composable components rather than trying to replace the runtime itself.

5. Formal Specification with TLA+

Connecting three independently developed systems introduces concurrency and coordination bugs that unit tests miss. Murat Demirbas’s “TLA+ Mental Models” (March 2026) lays out seven mental models for formal specification that map directly onto this integration boundary.

The Illegal Knowledge Problem

One recurring pitfall Demirbas highlights is writing guards that read global state atomically, assuming “illegal knowledge” no real process can have. Operon’s WatcherComponent currently reads shared_state atomically. In a single process that is safe, but in a distributed Swarms deployment no single watcher can take an atomic snapshot of stages running across separate nodes.

TLA+ refinement separates guards that are locally stable (modifiable only by the local organism’s own actions):

  • Own developmental stage: only the organism’s own tick() can advance it.
  • Own intervention count: only the local watcher increments it.
  • Append-only fact history: no action can delete a bi-temporal fact.

Other guards depend on remote state and require coordination:

  • Peer trust score: another organism’s record_outcome() can change it.
  • Library template rankings: other organisms’ record_run() calls shift scores.
  • Peer developmental stage: read during scaffold_learner(), where the cached value may be stale.

As Demirbas notes, “If you can make your guards locally stable, the protocol requires less coordination and tolerates communication delays gracefully.” Locally stable checks can run without distributed locking, so the inter-layer adapters should be designed around this split.

Safety Invariants

Any inter-layer adapter has to preserve six safety invariants:

  1. Template adoption safety: an organism never acquires a template with min_stage > its current developmental stage
  2. Trust monotonicity: trust scores only change via record_outcome(), never by direct mutation
  3. Bi-temporal append-only: facts are never deleted, only superseded
  4. Critical period irreversibility: once a critical period closes, it never reopens
  5. Convergence budget: intervention rate never exceeds threshold without triggering HALT
  6. Developmental monotonicity: stages never regress, even if telomeres are renewed

These invariants define the contracts for inter-layer adapters: a Swarms adapter importing an Operon template must preserve template adoption safety, and an AnimaWorks consolidation bridge must preserve bi-temporal append-only semantics. Model checking them in TLA+ verifies that they hold across all concurrent interleavings, which unit tests cannot exhaustively cover.

Atomicity That Needs Refinement

The current implementation assumes single-process atomicity everywhere. For distributed deployment, these assumptions break:

OperationCurrentDistributed
register_template()Atomic dict writeReplicated write with lag
consolidate()Single batchConcurrent organisms
on_stage_result()Atomic state writeAsync heartbeat signal
record_outcome()Atomic EMA updateConcurrent trust updates
import_from_peer()Atomic mutationNetwork partition mid-adoption

Standard TLA+ refinement starts from the coarse-grained atomic actions of the single-process implementation, splits them into finer steps, and reverifies safety at each step. Finer-grained actions give Swarms wider scheduling freedom and AnimaWorks higher heartbeat concurrency while the model checker confirms the safety invariants still hold.

Three Candidate Specs

  • TemplateExchangeProtocol.tla: models peer exchange with trust scoring to verify that adoption preserves the safety invariants.
  • DevelopmentalGating.tla: models the lifecycle and critical periods to verify that capability gating holds under concurrent adoption.
  • ConvergenceDetection.tla: models the intervention-rate signal across distributed organisms to verify that non-convergence is detected within bounded steps.

6. What Comes Next

With the eight-phase roadmap complete, the codebase stable, and the paper ready for publication, the remaining work is integration: building the adapters connecting Operon’s structural layer to AnimaWorks’ cognitive runtime and Swarms’ orchestration layer, backed by TLA+ specifications for the distributed boundaries.

Auditable memory, learning from operational history, and staged capability rollout are now in place inside the single-process library. The convergence work and TLA+ verification will test whether those guarantees survive the move from a standalone library into distributed production runtimes.
