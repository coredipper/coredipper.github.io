Harness Engineering as Categorical Architecture

Paper 5 is on arXiv. The Architecture triple (G, Know, Φ) formalizes Operon’s gates, certificates, and framework adapters under a single preservation theorem.
Bogdan Banu · May 13, 2026
paper-track post · arXiv:2605.12239

Five papers in, the object connecting Operon’s certificates, gates, compilers, and adapters finally has an explicit formal name. Paper 5: Harness Engineering as Categorical Architecture (arXiv:2605.12239, posted today) adopts the categorical Architecture triple (G, Know, Φ) from de los Riscos et al.’s ArchAgents framework and shows that the four pillars of agent externalization (Memory, Skills, Protocols, Harness) correspond directly to the components of that triple.

This post is a companion note to the paper, aimed at someone who reads the abstract and asks what it adds beyond the first four papers. Two things: it consolidates the per-paper engineering claims into one structural statement backed by a single preservation theorem, and it positions Operon explicitly against neighbour formalisms (typed lambda calculi, fixed-substrate frameworks, and output-validation harnesses).

The Architecture triple

G is the syntactic wiring: the directed graph of stage names and edges you see in a LangGraph spec, a Swarms GraphWorkflow, or an organism YAML. Colloquially, people call this “the architecture,” though on its own it constrains very little. Two systems with identical G can behave quite differently if their Know and Φ differ.

Know holds self-verifiable structural guarantees rather than stored facts: integrity gates, quality-based escalation rules, and supported convergence checks. Every certificate Operon emits (behavioral_stability_windowed, langgraph_state_integrity, dna_repair, dspy_compile_pinned_inputs, agentflow_evolve_pinned_inputs) lives in Know. The paper’s preservation theorem shows that Operon’s compiler functors preserve Know, so rewriting the graph does not invalidate the certificate predicate.

Φ is the interface mapping, a profunctor specifying how stages of a given mode (fast / fuzzy / deep) bind to model tiers. In current Operon, Φ is hand-coded in the compiler; a later version could learn it, provided the mapping preserves the contract that makes mode swaps safe.

With those three pieces in place, the four pillars of agent externalization map directly: Memory is coalgebraic state living over Φ, Skills are operad-composed objects in Know, Protocols are G, and the Harness is the full triple (G, Know, Φ). For two years I have been saying in talks and on Twitter that harness engineering is its own discipline; this triple gives that claim a precise definition.

Property preservation vs. neighbour formalisms

Neighbour formalisms address adjacent layers. Liu’s typed lambda calculus λA targets type safety and termination at the language layer, with empirical results showing that 94.1% of GitHub agent configurations are structurally incomplete under that formalization. Paper 5 operates one layer down, at compilation: where type safety checks that an expression’s shape is well-formed, property preservation checks that a certificate predicate’s truth value survives every compiler functor in Comp(Operon → X). Because the two layers compose cleanly, combining both formalisms in one project is a natural follow-up.

Operon’s specific guarantee is structural replay across harness rewrites: the compiler verifies both the identity of Know and replay of its certificate verifiers, leaving output-layer correctness, model behaviour, and readability outside its scope. That is narrower than claiming the agent will produce a correct answer, and stronger than claiming the program is well-formed. Because the terminal layer of an agent system is a non-deterministic model, the harness is the only layer where structural guarantees can actually be enforced.

Reference implementation: five targets, three certificates

The paper backs the correspondence with a reference implementation in the operon repo covering five external targets: Swarms, DeerFlow, Ralph, Scion, and LangGraph. Each target has a compiler functor (parse_X_topology() on the way in and organism_to_X() on the way out), plus compile_guarded_graph for the LangGraph-native execution path. Three certificate types are verified for preservation by identity and replay across all five targets: behavioral_stability_windowed, langgraph_state_integrity, and dna_repair.

The LangGraph compiler has a practical observability benefit. It creates one LangGraph node per organism stage and invokes the native runtime’s per-stage executor directly, avoiding a forked implementation. That gives you LangGraph-native tracing, LangSmith integration, and langgraph-cli compatibility without duplicating harness logic. Because the LangGraph rewrite is a compiler functor in Comp, the preservation theorem ensures that Know carries over and certificates emitted by the native runtime stay valid inside the LangGraph wrapper.

A small escalation experiment with real LLM agents (two models, one task) confirms that the quality-based escalation control path holds across a model swap, matching the substrate-independence prediction. The experiment is intentionally narrow in scope; the main contribution of the paper is the compilation framework and its preservation theorem.

Relation to earlier posts on pinecones and SLAM

Two earlier posts on this blog provide the cross-domain context the paper draws on. Pinecones and the Portable Certificate examines the Marom, Tibbits, Zardini, and Buehler preprint in materials engineering, which reaches the same compositional-verification pattern from a completely separate problem (biological mechanics translated to 4D-printed bilayer composites) and a physical validation methodology (substrate-validated rather than property-validated). Seeing the same structure arise in soft-matter engineering is good evidence that it is not an artifact of LLM agent tooling.

SLAM Already Solved Stagnation covers the underlying gate mechanism: Operon’s pre- and post-guards are a discrete-state port of factor-graph fixed-lag smoothing from robotics SLAM (Kaess et al., recently reframed by Dellaert as STAG). Taken together, SLAM supplies the mechanism, Marom et al. provide independent corroboration from materials engineering, and Paper 5 states and proves the preservation theorem.

Scope and non-claims

A few boundaries worth stating plainly before the formalism gets over-interpreted:

  • Output equivalence. Operon preserves recorded structural guarantees across compiler functors, without claiming that two harnesses with identical Know will produce identical model outputs. Only the certificate predicate survives the rewrite; model outputs remain non-deterministic.
  • Language-layer typing. Liu’s λA provides type safety and termination at the language layer, whereas Paper 5 provides property preservation at the compilation layer. The two frameworks address different layers and could be combined in a future system.
  • Output-validation harnesses. Guardrails AI and the validators-hub family inspect model outputs downstream, while Operon constrains the harness structure itself. Section 5 of the paper maps how the two layers sit side by side.
  • Generalisation from the escalation experiment. The empirical run covers two models, one task, and one escalation control path. It shows model-parametric behaviour in that setting; the case for the formalism rests on the compilation theorem rather than on extrapolating from that run.
  • Scope of operon-langgraph-gates. Version 0.1.0 ships StagnationGate and IntegrityGate, and that scope remains unchanged. Paper 5 clarifies why those two gates form the minimal non-trivial Know surface, without changing the v0.2 roadmap.

Practical consequences

First, cross-framework adapters like the agentflow L1+L2 pair shipped in v0.39 and the gascity adapter in v0.38 now fit into one picture as compiler-functor instances of preservation. The L2 hooks (Certificate.from_dspy_compile, Certificate.from_agentflow_compile) act as provenance-binding morphisms in a subcategory of Comp, with cheap recording-integrity checks and deferred re-execution checks sitting at opposite ends of the same morphism family. Integrating a new framework means adding another arrow to an existing diagram rather than specifying an ad-hoc contract.

Second, the (G, Know, Φ) vocabulary lets you compare agent frameworks by what they actually represent. A framework that exposes only G (such as LangGraph circa 2024) covers graph execution cleanly without making preservation claims. A system with (G, Know) and no Φ (early Operon) can preserve structural guarantees under fixed-mode execution, while mode swaps require an explicit interface mapping. Adding Φ completes the triple and allows model tiers to change without invalidating certificates. Most production stacks have no need for all three components, and stating that upfront keeps the taxonomy descriptive.

Third, operon-langgraph-gates v0.1.0 now has a published reference explaining why it starts with integrity and stagnation specifically: they are the two cheapest Know-component objects with non-trivial preservation properties under the LangGraph compiler functor. Having that argument on arXiv gives LangGraph users, framework authors, and collaborators a concrete specification to inspect.
